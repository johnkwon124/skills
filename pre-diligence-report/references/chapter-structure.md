# Report structure

Heading order:

```
Title            Company name and a one-line positioning
Heading 1        Before you read: minimum background
Heading 1        Executive summary
Heading 1        Ch 1. Why now: demand triggers and timing
Heading 1        Ch 2. Core technology: pain point, solution, how it works
Heading 1        Ch 3. [Variable] Key vertical or market deep dive
Heading 1        Ch 4. Competitive landscape
Heading 1        Ch 5. Program structure and revenue visibility
Heading 1        Ch 6. Team and IP
Heading 1        Ch 7. SWOT
Heading 1        Ch 8. Funding and deal dynamics
Heading 1        Ch 9. Strategic fit and catalyst calendar
Heading 1        Ch 10. Structural debates: N points that should shape the decision
Heading 1        Ch 11. Key diligence confirmations
Heading 1        Conclusion
```

Chapter 3 covers the company's most important or most contested market. Split it in two if there is no single such market.

| Section | Purpose | Min. words |
|---|---|---|
| Primer + executive summary | Primer: the problem in one sentence, why it matters now, 5 to 10 recurring terms defined, and the sourcing conventions (footnotes, "estimate" versus "analysis"). Summary: five one-paragraph answers (what the company does, why now, what to watch, structural debates, recommendation). The recommendation is conditional, such as "proceed to diligence subject to two or three confirmations" or "hold" | 600 |
| 1 Why now | Justify timing on three axes: demand, policy, capital. Quantified demand with sources, policy and capital pointing the same way, and company timing (last round, recent contracts) | 500 |
| 2 Core technology | Start with an analogy, then go to engineering. Pain point (limits of alternatives), solution, how it works (product lines, architecture), core physics, verified performance (sources first), key partnerships. No brochure tone. State who verified each performance figure | 800 |
| 3 Vertical deep dive | Why the problem is worst in this market, why the technology fits, the limits to face squarely (for example a large performance gap against the incumbent), and a conditional verdict | 600 |
| 4 Competition | Map competitors by camp: same idea with more capital, adjacent technologies, incumbents, and unconventional alternatives. State the target's relative position against each camp. One summary table is allowed | 700 |
| 5 Program structure and revenue visibility | Distinguish the nature of government or enterprise contracts: development funding versus production orders. Split revenue visibility into what can be calculated and what cannot. Quantify scenarios but do not invent revenue estimates | 500 |
| 6 Team and IP | Strengths and weaknesses of founders and key staff. Patent versus trade-secret posture and defensibility | 350 |
| 7 SWOT | Prose analysis of each element with evidence, not a four-box list | 400 |
| 8 Funding and deal dynamics | Round history, current round status (including unannounced), an estimated valuation range tagged "estimate" with comparable precedents, entry timing and competitive intensity | 400 |
| 9 Strategic fit and catalysts | Strategic fit for your organization (synergies, supply chain, customers) and a calendar of catalysts over 6 to 18 months (contract expiries, demos, rounds, regulatory events) | 350 |
| 10 Structural debates | The differentiating chapter. Three or four issues beyond surface facts, each as conventional view, counter-evidence or reinterpretation, implication for the investment decision. Examples: the real moat is data rather than hardware, a regulatory constraint read differently, a technology that is both opportunity and threat | 700 |
| 11 Diligence confirmations | A checklist of items to verify independently before formal diligence. Each item says what, why, and how. Derive them from the weakest links: single-source performance figures, revenue splits, regulatory exposure | 350 |
| Conclusion | Repeat the recommendation with the required confirmations, ending as a conditional judgment | 250 |

Typical finished length: 7,000 to 9,000 words, 15 to 20 pages, 30 or more footnotes.
