# Theme Memo Writer

## Purpose

Build a defensible point of view on one issue and connect it to sourcing. The memo is judged on depth of the issue, clarity of the competing hypotheses, and whether it produces a watch list.

- **Output:** a 4 to 6 page memo (PDF or Word) and a short summary message.
- **Cadence:** weekly at most. Skip a week rather than publish a weak memo.

Use it for issues, debates, and technical inflection points. Do not use it for a single-company write-up, a news digest, a raw signal scan, or a full sector map.

## Step 1. Pick the topic

1. Collect recent weak signals from your scans or reading notes. Count how often each keyword or theme recurred over the last two weeks.
2. Shortlist themes that recurred three or more times.
3. Offer three candidates in this format, then wait for a choice:

```
Subject: Theme memo candidates, pick one

1. {Topic A}
   Recurrences: {N} | Last seen: {date}
   Why it matters now: {one or two lines}
2. {Topic B} ...
3. {Topic C} ...
```

**Fallbacks when signals are thin:** (a) an issue you noted in recent meetings as "I need to understand this better", then (b) recent changes in areas adjacent to companies you already follow.

## Step 2. Write the memo

### 1. Issue definition (about 1 page)
- Why the topic surfaced now.
- The trigger: an event, paper, financing, or announcement.
- Who is paying attention: investors, companies, researchers.

### 2. Competing hypotheses (1 to 1.5 pages)
- **Consensus view:** what the market broadly believes.
- **Contrarian view:** the evidence against it.
- If possible, a third, emerging or niche view.

### 3. Evidence for each hypothesis (1.5 to 2 pages)
- Data, companies, papers, and market examples for each side.
- Cite a source for every claim. Label estimates "(estimate)". Mark unsourced numbers "(needs confirmation)". Never invent figures.
- Cite 5 to 10 companies. The aim is pattern recognition, not one company's story.

### 4. My take (about half a page)
- The most reasonable judgment given the evidence, and what would change it.
- What it implies for your own strategy or investment focus.

### 5. Watch list (about half a page)
- Indicators or companies to check next, tied to sourcing actions.
- Phrase each as "If this hypothesis is right, look at company X."

## Research method

- Run 5 to 10 searches per section.
- Prefer papers, patents, company announcements, investor essays, and technical blogs.
- Distinguish this memo from a company memo: it covers an issue rather than one company, uses a hypothesis structure, runs 4 to 6 pages, cites 5 to 10 companies, and is delivered as a PDF plus a short summary.

## Delivery

- File name: `theme_memo_YYYYMMDD_{topic_slug}.pdf`.
- Header: "Theme Memo | {date} | {topic}".
- Summary message: subject `Theme Memo: {topic}, {date}`, then three to five lines covering the issue, the core judgment, and the single most important watch-list item. Attach the PDF. If the attachment fails, paste the full text into the message body.
