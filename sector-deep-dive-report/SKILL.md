# Sector Deep-Dive Report

## When to use

Use this when the request covers a whole sector or technology (for example industrial robotics, grid-scale batteries, synthetic biology tooling) and the reader wants one connected argument: why the problem exists, how it is being solved, who competes, whether conditions are favorable, and where an investor should focus.

Do not use it for a single company or a single technology bet. That calls for a company memo. This report is about the shape of the whole field.

## Design principles

1. **Prose over boxes.** Put the analysis directly after the fact it explains. Callout boxes and tables separate analysis from evidence and slow the reader down. Use bullets only for real lists, such as the three approaches in section 2.
2. **Explain the business before the funding.** For each company, say what it does differently, what it sells, and who uses it. Funding goes last, in one or two sentences.
3. **Breadth in the landscape, depth in the profiles.** Map 8 to 12 companies briefly in the competition section. Profile only 3 to 5 in depth. Do not describe the same company twice.
4. **Cut what does not serve the argument.** A company already acquired gets one line. A company with a weak link to the sector's argument gets dropped.
5. **Prefer primary evidence.** Company-provided figures, interviews, and filings beat aggregator data. Use your own meeting or interview notes when you have them.

## Research protocol

Check every source type before writing:

1. Prior research files or earlier versions supplied by the user. Read them fully and treat them as a baseline to re-verify.
2. The current conversation. Reuse companies, comparisons, and points the user already raised.
3. Fresh web research. Source priority: (a) academic and institutional (arXiv, IEEE, IEA, consulting and bank research, Gartner), (b) specialist trade and deep-tech press, (c) general news, filings, and company investor pages.
4. Any notes from meetings or calls with companies in the sector. These outrank web sources when they conflict.
5. Papers, conference talks, and technical videos where a performance claim needs support.

Tag technology maturity with a Technology Readiness Level (TRL 1 to 9) where you can.

## Citation discipline

Tag claims while drafting, not afterward. Mark a figure the company announced itself as "(company-reported)". Mark a figure with no independent confirmation as "(unverified)". Example: "The company says its cells last 30% longer than competitors' (company-reported). No third-party benchmark has been published."

## Report structure

Use these sections in this order. Number and title them plainly ("1. Pain Point"). Each section is a few flowing paragraphs. Avoid tables.

1. **Pain Point.** Open with the size of the problem in concrete numbers. Before the statistics, say in one or two sentences why the problem matters now (a market, technology, or policy inflection) and why this technical approach is plausible. If you compare figures from different report editions, check that the change is real and not caused by a wider measurement scope.
2. **How to Solve.** The main solution approaches by type. Bullets are fine here.
3. **How it Works.** How the core technology operates, explained so a non-specialist can follow. Cite a specific paper or benchmark and one concrete number rather than a range. Add a TRL where possible.
4. **Competition.** Split into an incumbent axis (large public companies) and a private and startup axis. Cover 8 to 12 companies in one to three sentences each: technical approach, business model in a line, key customers or partners. This section carries breadth.
5. **Market Conditions.** Present favorable and unfavorable signals in balance, including debates such as valuation concerns or doubts about technical proof. End with where the weight of evidence sits and why.
6. **Regulation.** Rules and policy changes that affect the sector, and the direction of travel.
7. **Key Player Deep Dive.** Three to five companies. For each, in this order: (a) technical edge, with one sentence comparing it to others named in section 4; (b) core business, meaning what it sells and can deliver; (c) main customers and partners; (d) funding in one or two sentences. Point out tensions between operating metrics, for example headcount falling while revenue growth is projected to accelerate.
8. **Risks and Conclusion.** Sort each risk into (a) risks that time can resolve, such as technical maturation, regulatory clarity, or supply chain build-out, and (b) structural risks that persist, such as physical limits, capital intensity, or inherent competitive constraints. Then state an investment thesis for the sector and list the gaps that remain unverified.

Finish with a numbered **References** list covering every source, including those added during verification.

## Fact-check rounds

After drafting, run at least two verification passes:

- **Version and timing.** Are figures from different report editions presented as one trend? Could a wider scope or sample explain the change?
- **M&A and bankruptcy accuracy.** Confirm who actually acquired, who merely operates, who won, and who lost, using court documents or press releases.
- **Primary versus secondary conflicts.** If interview notes and an aggregator disagree, say which you used and why. Do not pick one silently.
- **Metric tension.** Do headcount, growth forecasts, and customer counts fit together?
- **TRL and citation check.** Every maturity claim has a TRL. Every study is named. No "research shows".
- **Inline tags.** Company-reported and unverified tags were applied while drafting.
- **Relevance.** Remove any company or paragraph that does not serve the argument.

Where possible, have an independent reviewer or fresh-context pass check the finished report against its sources before delivery.

## Output

- Body in flowing prose, with a cover page and the references list.
- Deliver a Word document and a matching PDF as a pair.
- Suggested file name: `deep_research_YYYY-MM-DD_<sector>_vN.docx` and `.pdf`.
- No fixed word count. Write what the topic needs.
- Convert with a headless office suite, then check the PDF text for layout or font problems.

## Workflow summary

1. Confirm the sector or technology and gather all five source types.
2. Draft the eight sections with TRL and inline tags from the start.
3. Verify in at least two rounds and fix errors immediately.
4. Confirm the structure: prose only, risks split into resolvable and structural.
5. Generate the document and PDF.
6. Run an independent check before delivery.
